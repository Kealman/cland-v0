# cland v0.1.2: Working with items

Status: complete (2026-10-12). Grilled with the human in the cland-design session on the release as a whole (tracker item T-228); each section is one decided ticket; the explorer cluster was charted as the wayfinder map T-264 (closed). The epics are tracker items labelled `v0.1.2` (section 4).

## 1. Purpose

v0.1.2 is "Working with items": the human works with items in cland as a full planning and tracking tool, not only watches status in the side panel. The core is the item explorer (T-243) with the human's item card (T-244), the release scope by epics (T-237, T-238), categories outside epics (T-245), id prefixes by kind (T-241) and done items distinct in the graph (T-242). Everything else is around that core.

## 2. Vocabulary

New terms in `GLOSSARY.md`: Explorer, Card, Summary, Category, Events view; Unscheduled narrowed to items with no container at all.

## 3. Decisions

### 3.1 Id prefixes per kind

Decided in T-241 on 2026-10-12.

- **The number is the identity, the prefix follows the kind:** `R-` release, `E-` epic, `T-` task, `Q-` question, `M-` map, `S-` spec, `C-` category once categories are a kind. One shared counter, so a number is unique across kinds and an item keeps its number when its kind changes.
- **Any prefix resolves.** The hub finds an item by its number whatever prefix is written, so `T-107` in the journal, in artifacts, topics and documents still leads to the epic the workbench draws as `E-107`. The CLI, the panels and the `gi` targets accept any prefix and display the kind's own.
- **Migration** is one command: it renames the item files and rewrites the references in frontmatter (`parent`, `blocked_by`); nothing else is rewritten.
- **Titles.** The migration also strips the kind word from titles (`Epic: X` becomes `X`; `Release: v0.1.2` becomes the release's name). The v0.1.2 release is titled "Working with items" and its version sits in the `version` field, so a release row reads `R-228 0.1.2 Working with items` (decided with the mockups, 2026-10-12).

### 3.2 Planned derives from the plan

Decided in T-238 on 2026-10-12.

- **With a plan** (v0.1 §3.10), the items of the epic's current stage are `planned` and the rest of the epic stays `backlog`; when the stage completes, the hub moves the next stage to `planned`, as it moves the other statuses on events.
- **Without a plan**, `planned` is set by hand by the Coordinator or the human, as before.
- **The release scope shows only the release's own items.** Other containers and unscheduled items are reached through the `all` filter and one count line at the bottom of the release scope.

### 3.3 Done items distinct from backlog

Decided in T-242 on 2026-10-12.

- **A done node** in the graph keeps its title readable: a green filled glyph and the title in a done colour (green-tinted, dimmer than live text), not the backlog grey; no strikethrough. The done colour is `#98bfa2` (the mockups, 2026-10-12).
- **Container nodes** (epics in a release graph, releases) carry `done/total` of their children and the count in work where task nodes carry the criteria fraction.
- **The same colour rule** applies to done rows in the items panel and on the board wherever done items are shown.

### 3.4 The verifier's item in the sessions panel

Decided in T-239 on 2026-10-12. The panel shows a verifier's held item with its role (built as T-168). When a verifier holds several items, the column shows the latest hold and `+N`. Before a hold exists, the item named by the latest review or QA request delivered to the session shows dimmed, until the hold replaces it.

### 3.5 Stopped sessions: resume, archive, cleanup

Decided in T-257 on 2026-10-12.

- **Resume.** `r` on a stopped row and the palette action "Resume session" relaunch the same harness session through the harness's own resume, keeping the cland name, role and claim; for this the hub records the harness session id at launch. When the harness cannot resume, the action says so and offers a fresh launch with the same brief.
- **Archive.** `d` on a session row archives it; `A` keeps showing or hiding the archive section. An archived session leaves every group and the default `session.list`; the journal keeps it.
- **Cleanup.** A stopped crew session (Implementer, Reviewer, QA) is archived automatically one hour after its item merged or closed, otherwise 24 hours after it stopped. The human's own sessions (Aides) never archive automatically. The background "more" fold shows the latest 20 stopped sessions and a count of the archived. `cland session prune --older-than <duration>` deletes the hub's records of archived sessions, never the journal.

### 3.6 The verification queue as the human sees it

Decided in T-246 on 2026-10-12. v0.1.2 adds no general queue API: `cland verify` (v0.1.1) is the queue for the suite, merges are serialized by the hub, and other resources wait for a need. The human-facing part: the `verify:` line in the sessions panel header becomes a one-line summary of the queue (who runs and for how long, how many wait) and opens the queue view (holder, waiters in order with their wait, the admission ETA from the median run). The human's own `npm test` goes through the same queue: the local runner does not start while the hub's queue is busy unless `--force` is given.

### 3.7 The inbox and the events view

Decided in T-247 on 2026-10-12 (the principle) and refined by the T-261 mockups the same day, accepted with their proposals P1 to P18.

- **The inbox holds the now layer only**, in four sections: asks and approvals (questions and command approvals); plan approvals, their own section placed second, because an Aide may approve a plan but not answer asks or commands; requests to the human (a message of the new kind `request`: a review request, a QA request, a handover); messages to the human (one row per now-layer message, in the sender's role colour; a topic waiting without an ask shows its latest message here). Each section is newest first, shows the last five, and folds the rest into an "N more" line that names the oldest wait; every row shows its waiting age. The selected row unfolds in place (an ask's options and free answer, a command in full); `r` replies from a row; `d` removes a row: a request is declined and its sender told, a message is marked read. `A` shows the inbox archive: what left the inbox in the last seven days and why. The empty state keeps the events line and the archive line.
- **The chip is the inbox count.** The hub's needsYou grows to the four kinds, each with its leave rule: answered, approved, replied or a verdict given, read or answered. The protocol gains the `request` send kind addressed to the human; a plan waiting under `[plan] approval = "human"` feeds the inbox.
- **Not-clear verdicts and session deaths** stay warn-level events (D17 of v0.1 §4); the placement table of v0.1 §3.5 is amended accordingly. An event a project override raises to now has no inbox row: it shows among the events view's warnings.
- **The events view.** `␣E` opens it from anywhere, `e` or Enter on the inbox's events line and the palette action too; `␣␣` goes back. Notifications and events newest first, grouped by day with day rules: the newest 200 rows that match the filter, `A` loads 200 older ones from the journal, `za` folds a day. A row is the time, a glyph, the kind, the subject and one line; Enter opens the subject (a card, a session, a topic, an artifact), `gi`, `gt` and `gs` jump, `gM` mutes the subject's events. Bookkeeping rows (launches, stops, claims, status moves, criterion marks, topic closes) are dimmed, and equal events on one subject within 60 seconds merge into one row with ×N; a red suite on `dev` is warn-level, on a task branch ordinary. Filter keys: `1` to `8` by kind (session, task, verdict, suite, release, artifact, usage, topic), `0` all, `w` warnings only, `s` a subject through the palette prefixes, `F` clears; the filter is kept in `ui.json`. Opening the view sets the human's last look; the inbox's events line, "△ N new events since your last look · ⚠ M", is in the watch colour. Nothing still waiting in the inbox repeats in the events view. The notification list of v0.1.1 with its `d` and `D` and its 50-row cap retires.
- **Records.** The boards InboxNow and Events on the design canvas, mirrored in `docs/design/main-screen/`; the v0 Inbox board is superseded.

### 3.8 The item explorer

Decided in the charting of the explorer map on 2026-10-12 (round 3; tickets T-243 and T-237).

- **Form.** An outline tree in the centre of the workbench with in-place folding, not columns. The root is the current release; below it, folded roots for the categories, the unscheduled items and the other releases. `l` and `h` unfold and fold (with `za`); `j` and `k` move between rows. The card (§3.9) in the right part follows the cursor; Enter moves the focus into it. `s` on a node rebases the tree on that node with breadcrumbs in the winbar; `␣␣` goes back.
- **Rows.** A release row: the version, epics done/total, the status breakdown. An epic row: tasks done/total, the counts in progress, in review and in qa as a compact bar, the stage in work, the lead. A task row: the status glyph, the id, the title, the claim or holder, the latest verdict, the criteria fraction. Inside a container the live items come in rank order and the done items after them in the order they closed, in the done colour (§3.3). `D` hides done items everywhere, kept in `ui.json`.
- **Read-only.** The explorer edits nothing by hand: the human decided on 2026-10-12 that hand edits would add chaos and that steering through the Coordinator is the convenient way. The one action on an item, or on a visual selection of items, is a quick message into the item's topic addressed to the Coordinator ("fix the parent", "plan these", "take this next"), composed with the item context and the selected ids; the Coordinator acts through the tracker. The board's rank keys from v0.1 and the plan approval (`gp`) stay as they are.
- **Keys and place.** `␣e` opens the explorer; it is the centre view at startup when nothing else is open. `gi` opens the explorer with the card on the item, replacing the item view. The board (`␣b`), the graph (`␣G`) and the plan view (`gp`) stay.
- **Accepted mockups (T-259, 2026-10-12, proposals P1 to P22).** The one action key is `m`: a row in normal mode, a selection in visual mode. A message about one item goes to the item's topic; a message about a selection goes to General, with the scope and one line per item (id, kind, status, container, rank, title) shown as the Coordinator receives them. An epic's glyph and status derive from its tasks (closed ●; any task in progress to ready ◐; planned ○ in text colour; otherwise ○ dim), the release row's from its epics; the progress bar has one cell per child in status order, with the in-progress, review and qa counts in their own columns; the lead column reads the Coordinator while `leads` is empty. An epic with a plan groups its live tasks under one label per stage and its done tasks under one "done" label, in the order they closed. A task row shows its claim, or the verifier's hold while in review or qa; a done row shows when it merged; a verdict carries its round. The folded roots name what they hold; "Other releases" lists open releases only, closed ones through the archive toggle. Direct tasks of a release sit under a folded "Direct tasks (N)" row after the epics. `D` hides done items in the whole explorer tree, done epics included, and the header shows what it hides. The planned glyph is ○, as in v0.1 §3.11 and the build; the ◔ of the older boards retires.
- **The prototype's answers (T-262, 2026-10-12; the human tried the prototype on `prototype/explorer` and accepted it).** A release with no epics keeps its direct tasks folded, as any other release. When the centre is narrow (both side panels open at 200 columns leave 57 columns to the tree), the card takes the right panel's place. Two message keys: `m` sends to General, with the item context riding along; `M` creates the item's topic when it has none and sends there (a topic bound to a task or an epic, as the Unit of work allows); on a selection both go to General. Messages name items by their display id (the kind prefix) once the migration has run, by the hub's id before. `l` on a leaf focuses the card; `h` on a leaf or a folded node goes to the parent; `s` on a leaf scopes to its container; `j` and `k` move over visible rows. The header's done count is the count hidden in the current scope. Maps sit under Unscheduled for now. `A` toggles the archived roots (closed releases, closed root items). The v0.1 item view stays reachable on `gI` and in the palette for the human's criteria and label edits of v0.1 §3.4; the card itself has no edit keys. The explorer rebuilds its model on every hub event and redraws only on a visible change; the expectation is under 15 milliseconds for a tree of 300 items, with lazy folds a later concern.
- **Records.** The mockups on the design canvas (Explorer, ExplorerEpic, Categories), mirrored in `docs/design/main-screen/`, and the Neovim prototype on `prototype/explorer` (`nvim/EXPLORER-PROTOTYPE.md`: keys, choices, measurements, the recorded screen) are the reference.

### 3.9 The item card and the summary

Decided on 2026-10-12 (round 3; T-244).

- **Summary.** A `## Summary` section of one to three sentences, written by whoever creates the item: the `to-tickets` and `plan-epic` skills, the Coordinator's and the Implementer's clauses, the human. When it is missing, the fallback, refined by the T-258 research (87.5 percent of the existing items read as summaries with it, against 63 percent for the plain first paragraph): the first section that is not Parent, Notes, Sources, References, Acceptance criteria or Definition of done; in it, the first prose paragraph with its leading provenance sentences dropped, or the first list item when there is no prose; trimmed to two whole sentences and 50 words, without a trailing lead-in that ends in a colon. The rule runs on the parsed body, never on the raw file.
- **The card.** The summary; the facts (status, rank, layer, labels, size (§3.12), the parent path, lead, claim, holds); the progress (the children's breakdown for a container, the criteria with their marks for a task); the plan stage for an epic; the latest verdict with its one-line reason and its scope (§3.12); blocked by, blocks and `regression_of` (§3.12); the branch and the latest `cland verify` result for a task; created, updated and the last activity. The agents' text (the body) sits behind `zb`, unchanged for the agents. A container has no status of its own (v0.1 §3.2): its glyph, status line and layer derive from its children (the mockups' proposal P2 and P15, 2026-10-12).
- **Place.** The card is the right part of the explorer and replaces the item view on `gi`.
- **Accepted mockups (T-260, 2026-10-12).** Enter focuses the card and its header lists the keys that work there; `h` or `␣␣` returns to the tree. The composer stays one line on the card until `m` opens it in place; the item and the card line under the cursor ride along. The agents'-text fold line names the body's sections and its size. A container's card lists the claims and holds on its tasks; its latest verdict and last activity come from its subtree and name the item; its layer is the highest among its tasks. The task card adds the branch and the latest `cland verify` result. The boards CardEpic and CardTask are the reference.
- **The prototype's answers (T-262).** The card keeps a Decision block (the latest resolution or decision comment) for items that carry one, and the summary's dimmed provenance line ("fallback from ## X") so that a missing `## Summary` is visible. The row shows an item's ordinal within its container; the raw rank appears among the facts only when set by hand. Last activity is the later of the item's `updated` time and its topic's latest message, naming which. The hub supplies what the card needs without a `task.view` per row: the closing time on the item summary, verified/total in `task.list`, the latest verify result per task, and the §3.12 fields; closing an epic closes its plan's stages.

### 3.10 Categories

Decided on 2026-10-12 (round 3; T-245).

- **A container kind** `category` at the root level beside releases, a root kind in `[tracker] levels`. The migration (§3.1) seeds Tech debt (from T-216), Bugs, Follow-ups and Ideas; `cland task create --kind category` adds more. An item in a category is not unscheduled: unscheduled means no container at all.
- **Agents** put an item into a category with `--parent C-n`; the clauses say when: a bug found aside, tech debt, a follow-up. The `tech-debt` label retires with the migration.
- **The explorer** shows the categories as a root section with counts. Promotion is a message to the Coordinator (§3.8): move into an epic or a release, rank to the top, plan these.
- **Idle work.** The Coordinator takes category work in rank order when the release has nothing unblocked, from the categories named by `idle_from` in `crew.toml`; empty means never.
- **Accepted mockups (T-259).** An unfolded category shows its top three items by rank, then "N more", with its open count and how many done items `D` hides; a category named in `idle_from` carries an "idle work" mark.

### 3.11 The side panel at release scope

Decided on 2026-10-12 (round 3; T-237). At release scope the items panel lists the release's epics as folded rows with done/total and the status breakdown, in rank order; an unfolded epic shows its status sections as the epic scope does today; the epic tab row stays for narrowing to one epic; the categories appear as one count line at the bottom (§3.2). Direct tasks of the release sit under a folded "Direct tasks (N)" row after the epics (decided 2026-10-12 with the mockups).

### 3.12 Per-model task performance: the instrumentation

Decided in T-255 on 2026-10-12, on the research `research/model-performance` (commit 53b449d). The v0.1 runs cannot rank models: from 2026-10-10 on every Implementer and Reviewer session was gpt-6.1-sol and every QA session gpt-6-astra, and task size, the run and the Reviewer session move the numbers more than the model.

- **Scope in v0.1.2: instrumentation only.** The human measures later, on another project with many models; the v0.1.2 crew stays as §4 says, with no model rotation in this build.
- **What the hub records.** Session usage in the journal at each `session.usage` publication and at stop (tokens and cost or rate-limit share per session, hence per role and per item). A required `size` field on tasks: an ex-ante size class set by whoever files the ticket; the skills and the clauses ask for it. A `regression_of` link from a task to the task it reworks, set by the author or the Coordinator when filing. The model of every launch, sub-agents included, in the launch record, never empty when the harness knows it. The scope of a review verdict (full, delta, evidence-only) on the verdict record.
- **The metric set**, for the later measurement: first-pass yield (the first full-scope review clear), rounds to the first clear review, reviewer-found defects by severity with suite failures apart, escaped defects through `regression_of`, cost per landed task for the Implementer and for verification; compared only within the same size class, run and Reviewer model. The retrospective of a run shows what the records allow.
- **Records.** `docs/research/model-performance.md` with `extract.py` and its CSVs.

### 3.13 Accounts and limits

Decided in T-240 on 2026-10-12, on the research `research/account-limits` (commit f8b549e).

- **The registry** lives at user level in `~/.config/cland/accounts.toml`: names and paths only, never a secret. `roles.toml` and launches refer to an account by name; the account is recorded on the session; every process the adapter starts or queries for a session, `agents --json` included, runs in the account's environment (`CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, `CODEX_HOME`, `XDG_DATA_HOME` or `OPENCODE_DB`).
- **Limits data** comes from running sessions first: Codex's `account/rateLimits/updated`, Claude Code's status-line `rate_limits` forwarded by the cland mod. Idle accounts are polled at most every 15 minutes and only through the harness itself (`claude -p "/usage"`, a short-lived `codex app-server`); cland never calls a provider's endpoint with a token it reads; OpenCode exposes nothing and shows nothing.
- **The panel** gets an accounts block at the top of the sessions panel: per account, the windows the source sent (5-hour, weekly, per-model weekly) with the used share and the reset time; a limited account is highlighted.
- **Routing without rotation.** An account is pinned to a role or to a launch by hand. On a limit-hit signal (Claude `rate_limit` and 429, Codex `usageLimitExceeded`, OpenCode `provider.quota`) the hub marks the account limited, refuses new launches on it and asks the human to pick another. No automatic failover or round-robin: the providers' terms forbid rotating accounts and multi-account circumvention, and a second paid account for overflow is the human's own call.
- **Records.** `docs/research/account-limits.md`; T-265 (the Claude adapter's polling near token expiry, claude-code#95822) precedes multi-account use.

### 3.14 The remote executor

Decided in T-256 on 2026-10-12, on the prototype `prototype/remote-executor` (commit 16ae91a, `docs/research/remote-executor.md`): the suite ran on a DigitalOcean c-8 droplet, 1612 of 1623 with `PS_PERSONALITY=bsd`, ready to run 86 seconds after the create call, the suite 12 to 23 percent slower than local, about $0.07 per scripted run, peaks of 95 processes and 694 threads, 77 thousand short-lived process exits per run.

- **The executor** is cland's own orchestration behind a provider-agnostic interface: create from an image, wait for SSH or exec, deliver the tree as a bundle together with the hub's suite selection (`CLAND_SUITE_SELECTION`, since a bundle carries no `origin/dev`), install, run, collect, destroy; a lifetime watchdog, an orphan sweep by tag and a spend cap. A pre-built image or snapshot replaces cloud-init provisioning. Remote runs have their own budget beside the local cap of one.
- **The first backend is droplets** (decided on 2026-10-12 on the MicroVM research, `docs/research/do-microvms.md` on `research/do-microvms`): DigitalOcean MicroVMs are a public preview a few days old in one region (MKC1), without SSH or a public IP, with exec capped at 60 seconds per command and a team-wide exec budget, pid limits unpublished, and slightly dearer per run ($0.068 against $0.054 on a `c-8` droplet). Their advantages, warm checkpoint clones, free creation and auto-pause, make them the second backend behind the same interface once one experiment passes: one suite run on an 8 vCPU MicroVM, about $0.12, the script in the research note. Fly Machines third. The executor interface is built on acquire, deliver, prepare, start, status, log, cancel, release and reap, not on SSH, so a backend without SSH fits.
- **Local and remote stay switchable** (the human's addendum of 2026-10-12, through cland-impl): never a hard binding. The executor is a per-run choice (`cland verify --executor local|cloud`, or the session's request) with a per-project default in config; the human picks remote when a release is urgent and local when there is no hurry. The scheduler, the result cache and the reporting are the same on both paths; a remote failure to acquire a machine reports as such and does not silently fall back.
- **Before the executor:** the suite's own spend (T-267: the 36 thousand `git` and 20 thousand `ps` spawns per run are measured and cut where there is room) and T-266 (the hub and the suite portable to Linux).

The map's prototypes refine §3.7 to §3.11; their reactions are recorded here by the map's final ticket.

## 4. The release

Decided on T-228 on 2026-10-12 (round 1).

- **Goal.** As section 1; the release is named "Working with items".
- **Scope.** Everything under T-228 is in v0.1.2. T-248 (the key-encoding matrix in the human's terminals) is the human's own task, not crew work; on 2026-10-12 it moved out of the release under Tech debt (T-216), since v0.1 §3.1 refuses a release with an open child.
- **Epics**, in the tracker as items labelled `v0.1.2` and `epic`, children of T-228:
  1. T-268 Suite spend and Linux portability (§3.14, before the executor: T-267 and T-266).
  2. T-269 Prefixes and categories (§3.1; §3.10, the hub side; the items panel's count line of §3.2 and §3.11).
  3. T-270 Status, fields and instrumentation (§3.2; the hub's fields for the card in §3.9; §3.12).
  4. T-271 Remote executors (§3.14, with the MicroVM experiment as the second backend's gate).
  5. T-272 Accounts and limits (§3.13, with T-265).
  6. T-273 Release v0.1.2 from the item (T-253 and T-254; the release, its post-release check and the retrospective).
  7. T-274 Inbox and the events view (§3.7).
  8. T-275 Sessions: stopped sessions, verifiers' items, the queue line (§3.4, §3.5, §3.6).
  9. T-276 The explorer and the card (§3.3, §3.8, §3.9, §3.10 the Neovim side, §3.11).
- **Order.** Two lanes run in parallel: the hub lane T-268 → T-269 → T-270 → T-271 → T-272 → T-273 and the Neovim lane T-274 → T-275 → T-276 → T-273; T-276 also waits for T-269, and T-273, the release, waits for the ends of both lanes. The suite's own spend and Linux portability come first, so the remote executors after them run the rest of the build's suites on cloud machines. The blocking edges are on the items.
- **Fog and AFK work.** One wayfinder map for the explorer cluster (mockups on the canvas and a Neovim prototype, as in v0.1). In parallel, AFK: a prototype run of the suite on a DigitalOcean 8 vCPU VM (T-256, branch `prototype/remote-executor`), research on harness limits and account selection (T-240, `research/account-limits`), research extracting per-model task data from the journal (T-255, `research/model-performance`).
- **Secrets.** The DigitalOcean token lives in the human's shell environment (`DIGITALOCEAN_TOKEN`); the prototype reads it and never stores it; its VMs are tagged and swept.
- **D23 accepted.** Detection and the bounded stop stay as shipped in v0.1.1; the fail-fast exit is T-253, ordinary tech debt, not raised.
- **Crew.** As in v0.1.1: Implementers on Codex gpt-6.1-sol, Claude Code roles on sonnet, Reviewer pool 2; plan approval `human` for the explorer epic only, `none` for the rest.
- **D24 (2026-10-12 07:40Z).** The stand-in cland-impl answers every ask of this build on the human's behalf, the explorer epic's plan approval included (`docs/spec/v0.1.md` §4, D24).
- **D25 (2026-10-13 10:50Z).** The human, in two parts (`docs/spec/v0.1.md` §4, D25):
  - Implementers run `cland verify` only after a clear review verdict, at the exact reviewed head. A fix after a verdict needs a new review before a new run.
  - Linux proof and diagnostic runs may use a c-8 DigitalOcean droplet through T-282's driver, beside the local container slot. That driver becomes T-271's first backend.
  - The droplet rules: the `cland` tag, a lifetime of at most 30 minutes, a watchdog, destroy on exit, an ordinary user, and a token never printed. At most one cland droplet is alive at a time, and spend is capped at $2 a day.
  - Each run posts its create, destroy and cost on its item. The stand-in checks the tag hourly and alerts the human only past 70 minutes or at the cap.
- **Definition of done.** As v0.1.1 (D16, D19 of v0.1 §4): each epic's demo run by QA on `dev` with a clear verdict, v0.1.2 released with `cland release --item T-228`, a retrospective artifact of the run.

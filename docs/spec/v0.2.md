# cland v0.2: the web workbench

Status: draft (2026-10-14). Charted by the v0.2 wayfinder map (tracker item M-399) in the cland-design session from the human's direction of 2026-10-14; each resolved ticket adds or rewrites its section. It becomes complete when the map's cut ticket closes and the v0.2 epics are published under the release item R-377. The v0.1 and v0.1.2 specs stay in force where this one is silent.

## 1. Purpose

v0.2 brings the web workbench: everything the Neovim workbench shows and does, in the browser, more convenient and without the limits of a console interface. The hub is the single source of truth; the web and Neovim are two clients of one protocol. Neovim stays as the alternative: good for a fast start, for setting up the process and for driving the harnesses; once a project's work has settled and scaled, the human moves to the browser to watch, plan and steer.

## 2. Vocabulary

New terms land in `GLOSSARY.md` as they are decided (candidates: Web workbench, Client, Snapshot, Delta, Rail, Drawer).

## 3. Decisions

Settled while charting, on 2026-10-14 (two grilling rounds; the map's tickets refine them).

### 3.1 Clients and transport

- **Two clients, one hub.** The hub serves the web client itself: HTTP for the static bundle and a WebSocket carrying the same frame protocol the Neovim client speaks (the Q-400 research: one dispatcher already serves the spokes' WebSocket, the unix socket and stdio; the 37 requests and 21 pushed frames Neovim uses carry over as they are), with the identity `human`. The browser's entry is a one-time token printed by `cland web`, exchanged for a cookie; the hub's secret never leaves the machine's own processes (corrected on 2026-10-14 by the research: the spokes' port refuses client hellos on purpose). No separate backend, no database of the web's own.
- **Several human clients at once.** The hub accepts several `human` connections (Neovim and any number of browsers; the research found nothing limits them today); all see the same state. The web keeps its own interface state in the browser; `ui.json` stays Neovim's.
- **Where it runs.** Localhost and the local network first, phones and tablets included; remote access through the human's own tunnel or VPN, never a cloud service.

### 3.2 The shell

A persistent left rail of areas (Inbox, Items, Sessions, Topics, Events, Artifacts, Accounts); a main area that can split into two panes; a drawer on the right for the card or a thread. Board, Graph and Plan are not areas but views of the Items area beside the tree (the explorer): a view switcher in the area's header keeps the scope, the filter and the selection when the view changes, and Plan shows the plan of the selected epic (the human's reaction on the web canvas, 2026-10-14: the board and the graph are different views of the items, not something separate). Keyboard first, as in Neovim: the palette on ⌘K, the same letters where they fit, `?` shows the keys.

The Topics area lists topics in groups: what waits for the human first, then the standing topics of the project (General, Agents) set apart with their own look, then the topics opened under items, then the closed ones folded (the human's reaction on the web canvas, 2026-10-14: standing and item topics must be told apart at a glance).

Every group and block in the web folds, with a chevron and the keyboard, and its folded state is remembered per client in the browser's own interface state: the session groups and the accounts block, the inbox sections, the explorer roots and containers, the topics groups (the human's reaction on the web canvas, 2026-10-14).

No explanatory prose in the interface: headers carry a name and a count, rows carry facts; what a block means and how it is fed lives behind `?`, in tooltips and in the docs (the human's reaction on the web canvas, 2026-10-14: "избыточное информирование").

**Accepted boards (Q-404, 2026-10-14).** The five boards on the web canvas (the shell, the thread, the inbox, the sessions, the phone) are the reference with their proposals P1 to P25: the rail lists the areas as words with counts only where something asks for attention, and collapses to a column of icons with badges; the top strip holds the release switcher, the palette on ⌘K, the Coordinator, the needs-you pill and the hub dot; Golos Text for prose (for its Cyrillic) and JetBrains Mono for ids, code and data, sentence-case headings; the keyboard cursor is a focus ring and hover only lightens a row; ⌘\ splits the main area, panes swap and close, the drawer stays closed while split; the drawer has Split and Close and stays closed when nothing is selected; the Topics area has its own list; the Accounts area holds the registry and the limits (v0.1.2 §3.13) while Sessions keeps a compact strip of account meters; past 16 children a container shows a proportional bar instead of cells; the card's rank fact appears only when set by hand. Keys match by physical key (`KeyboardEvent.code`) so the Russian layout works; ids never wrap at the hyphen; the role colours are the build's (`hl.lua`), and the text grey is raised to 4.5:1.

### 3.3 The live model

Decided in Q-407 on 2026-10-14 (after the Q-400, Q-402 and Q-403 research).

- **Snapshot, deltas, resume.** On a client hello the hub sends a snapshot (items, sessions, topics, the inbox, the first page of events, accounts) with an epoch and a sequence number; every pushed delta carries the next number. A reconnecting client sends `resume` with its epoch and number and receives the missed deltas from the hub's ring buffer, or a fresh snapshot when the ring no longer covers them or the epoch changed (the ring is in memory and a hub restart starts a new epoch). The history of threads and events is served in pages by message number. Neovim adopts the extension in 0.2.x.
- **Computed once in the hub, for both clients:** the unread counts, the merged session rows, the explorer roll-ups and the summary rule, the events filter, and the last look and read marks per human. The wire types live in a module that imports nothing from the hub, so a browser build stays free of Node globals.
- **One gate for answers.** An ask or an approval has one record in the hub; the first answer from any client wins and every client sees the outcome.

The Q-405 prototype (`prototype/web-shell`, 2026-10-14) measured why a client's first full tree takes about two seconds today: eleven reads queued on the hub's event loop at connect (1.15 MB, `hub.status` alone 336 kB of which 308 kB is suite history, `task.list` 46 percent verdict history), the verification queue pushed about once a second whole, and `task` frames carrying the layer of every item. The snapshot therefore carries the tracker levels and not the suite history, replaces the reads at connect, and a delta carries only what changed; the verification queue is pushed only when it changes; the card's facts come in the snapshot and the deltas, not from a `task.view` per card and a `task.plan show` per epic row. The frame-to-screen latency was 2.5 ms median once the model is loaded, so the browser's own work is not the cost. The web self-hosts its two fonts.

On connection the hub sends a snapshot, then deltas with sequence numbers so that a reconnecting client catches up instead of re-reading everything; the history of threads and events is served in pages. This is a protocol extension Neovim may use later: today pushed frames carry no sequence numbers, nothing is replayed, Neovim re-reads eight lists and every open thread after a drop, clients have no ping and the edge cuts a socket silent for 60 seconds (Q-400). The research sizes the pieces at about nine sessions (the web edge on a stable port with server pings, the token and cookie with Origin and Host checks, frames serialized once, the snapshot, deltas with `seq` and an epoch, history pages, static serving, artifact files over HTTP) plus three for fields both clients need computed in the hub (unread counts, merged session rows, explorer roll-ups and the summary rule, the events filter) and read marks held in the hub instead of `ui.json`. The grilling ticket Q-407 decides the shape, and reserves what a later terminal needs (Q-403): text frames are JSON and binary frames are reserved for streams; streams sit beside the live model and never enter its sequence numbers, snapshot, catch-up or the journal; sequence numbers count model deltas, not socket frames; each connection has a queue limit past which the model stream resyncs; model frames go first or terminals get their own socket; the entry is routed by path, the cookie serves any number of sockets, every upgrade checks its Origin; each frame kind has its own size limit; the hello carries a feature list and each adapter a `terminal` capability.

### 3.4 Threads and actions

Decided in Q-408 on 2026-10-14 (after the Q-401 research and the accepted boards).

- **An ask lives in two places with one state.** The inbox row and the card in the thread show the same record from the hub, answer with the same keys (options 1 to 9 and a free answer), decline with a reason on its own key and never on Escape; once answered, the card folds to one line with the outcome in both places.
- **Folded runs** open in place and keep their anchor line, never in the drawer; the fold line is built by the hub from facts (who, how many messages, the latest step, the age), never written by a model.
- **The composer.** Enter sends a one-line message, Shift+Enter breaks the line, ⌘Enter always sends; on a touch keyboard return breaks the line and only Send sends; a message to a busy session is queued and shown in the thread as queued with a Withdraw that puts the text back into the composer; ⌘K in the composer still opens the palette; the draft is kept per topic in the browser and survives a disconnection and a reload, with Send disabled while disconnected.
- **Declining an ask** (decided with the phone boards, Q-406) is an answer with the value `declined` and a reason: the asker receives it as any answer, the topic stays open and may ask again; there is no separate "ask me later". An answered ask folds in place to one line with the option, who answered and when, in the thread for good and in the inbox until the human leaves the inbox, then it goes to the inbox archive (this refines the leave rule of v0.1.2 §3.7, so rows never move under a finger).

Markdown, role colours and folded agent runs as v0.1 §3.14; artifacts inline, HTML in a sandboxed iframe; asks and approvals as cards with option buttons on keys 1 to 9 and a free answer, an answered ask folding to one line in place; a folded agent run as one row with a live one-line summary; an HTML artifact inline at 240 px in a sandboxed iframe (never `allow-scripts` with `allow-same-origin`); a composer that chooses Message, Decision or Question with the addressee as a chip; the same addressing rules as Neovim (v0.1.2 §3.8: `m` to General with the item context, `M` to the item's topic, a selection to General).

### 3.5 Editing: parity with Neovim

In 0.2.0 the web edits nothing by hand that Neovim does not: the explorer's action is the message to the Coordinator, plan approval, the criteria edits of the item view. Dragging rank or moving items between epics with the mouse is fog for 0.2.x.

### 3.6 Notifications and the phone

The needs-you count in the tab title and browser notifications while the page is open; at phone width the inbox, the sessions, the threads and answering asks work, with a bottom bar of Inbox, Topics, Sessions and Events in place of the rail and an ask opening as a bottom sheet; the planning views are desktop first; a PWA manifest so the page installs on a phone. Decided with the phone boards (Q-406, 2026-10-14, proposals P26 to P42): the bottom bar is Inbox, Topics, Sessions, Events and More, where More opens a small sheet with Items, Artifacts and Accounts read-only; a session row is two lines and the list has no row or swipe actions (Stop, Resume, Archive and Capabilities live on the session's page with confirmations); the accounts block is a strip of cells showing each account's worst window; the queue view opens as a full-height sheet; a topic opens full screen with a back control; an ask sheet rides on the keyboard with 44 px options and Decline on the field's label row; the inbox is home with 44 px fold headers and "Nothing needs you" when every section is at 0; when the socket drops a strip says since when and when the next retry runs, the last snapshot stays on screen with its time, answers, approvals and Send are disabled and nothing is queued, and on reconnect the client resumes from its sequence number; an inline HTML artifact is a chip that opens full screen on the phone. Plan approval needs the plan view and stays on the desktop: the phone shows a waiting plan as a row and notifies; the approvals that stay in a harness TUI (§3.8) show on the phone marked and notified, never answerable there. The notification switches and the push subscription button (which iOS requires on a tap) live in More on the phone and beside the profile in Accounts on the desktop. The last look, the read marks and what counts as read are held in the hub per human, not per client, so Neovim and every browser agree (decided 2026-10-14, Q-407). Notifications fire only for items of the now layer and never while the human is looking at the page; separate switches for "needs you" and "finished"; a notification names the session, the item and what it waits on, and a click opens the inbox row; on a phone all of it waits for HTTPS through the human's tunnel (Q-408).

### 3.7 Serving and security

Decided in Q-407 on 2026-10-14: the web edge is a separate listener inside the hub process on `ws`, on a stable loopback port recorded in `hub.json`, serving `/`, `/auth` and `/ws`; `cland web` prints a one-time link (with `--qr`, as a QR code for a phone; the link carries the tunnel's address when `[web]` names one, else the local network's) and the browser trades its token for an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie named per port that lives 30 days (as `opencode pair` does); on iOS an installed app keeps its cookies apart from Safari, so the link opens in the installed app or the sign-in is repeated there; every upgrade checks Host and Origin and only a client hello is accepted, so a page can never connect as a session. The cookie carries the human's authority except four actions that stay in the CLI and Neovim in 0.2.0: stopping and restarting the hub, releasing, and creating artifacts from arbitrary paths of the machine; the web does not offer them.

Refined by the Q-402 research (2026-10-14): the `web/` workspace is React 19 with Vite and TypeScript strict on the hub's own flags, CSS Modules on the canvas tokens as custom properties, no UI library (22 packages; the bundle about 70 kB gzipped; an AGENTS.md rule and a check against hex colours keep agents on the tokens); the wire types move into a module that imports nothing from the hub, so the browser's typecheck stays free of Node globals; the web edge is a separate listener inside the hub process on `ws` (already a dependency; Node 24 has no WebSocket server) serving the bundle, `/auth` and `/ws`, about 16 kB and one file handle per client, a message to 51 clients in 11 ms; the browser trades the one-time token for an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie named per port and only a client hello is accepted; the web phase of the suite runs as its own machine-locked shard after the hub phase on `node:test` with `playwright-core` and the headless shell (2.6 s fixed plus 0.1 to 0.2 s per page test and 2 to 4 s per test that drives a fake harness; about 30 tests add 15 to 40 s to a full run; selection runs it only for `web/**` or protocol changes); the PWA needs a manifest and two icons; notifications with the page open are small, on a phone they need HTTPS through the human's tunnel and a service worker, and with the page closed they go through the browsers' push services, a policy question outside 0.2.0.

A `web/` workspace built into `web/dist`, served by the hub on a stable loopback port (one canonical origin; the cookie named per project, since cookies are not isolated by port); `cland web` prints a link with a one-time token and opens the browser, a cookie afterwards; the network only through `[web] bind` (an amendment of ADR 0003, with Origin and Host checks against cross-site WebSocket hijacking); the web phase of the suite as its own shard in headless Chromium. Open for Q-407: a web cookie carries the human's full authority today (`hub.stop`, release, artifacts from any readable path) and may need an allowlist; an HTTP origin on the LAN is not a secure context, so the phone's notifications and the PWA (§3.6) need HTTPS or localhost.

### 3.8 Scope of 0.2.0

Watching, planning and steering through messages: every area of §3.2 including writing to a session or the Coordinator, answering an ask, approving a command or a plan, reading a verdict and an artifact. A harness terminal in the browser (xterm.js) is not in 0.2.0: a session in the web is its thread, what the hub knows; the terminal is a later theme the protocol must not preclude (Q-403: every harness has an attach command the hub could run in a PTY of its own and relay to xterm.js; Claude Code first, then Codex, OpenCode last; a 0.2.x theme decided on how often the human still goes to Neovim for TUI-only actions). Approving a command from the browser (decided in Q-407): the hub relays approvals where the harness's protocol allows it, the Codex app-server and OpenCode's permission API; for Claude Code the approval stays in the TUI until its supervisor exposes it, and the web marks such rows "answer in Neovim". One gate in the hub: the first answer from any client wins.

## 4. The release

Decided in the cut ticket Q-409 on 2026-10-14.

- **Scope.** Everything in section 3 is in v0.2.0: the shell with its seven areas; Items with the tree, the card and the Board, Graph and Plan views; Topics with threads, asks, approvals and the composer; Inbox; Events; Sessions; Artifacts; Accounts; the protocol extension (the web edge, the token and the cookie, the snapshot and the deltas, history pages, the hub-computed fields and the read marks per human, the approvals relay for Codex and OpenCode); notifications with the page open, the phone layouts, the PWA manifest and the QR sign-in; the web phase of the suite. About 50 sessions on the v0 scale.
- **Deferred to 0.2.x:** a harness terminal in the browser; direct edits with the mouse (drag to rank, move between epics); a home page over several projects; push notifications with the page closed; Neovim adopting the snapshot and the deltas; a read-only view for another person.
- **Epics**, in the tracker as items labelled `v0.2` and `epic`, children of R-377 (filled when published):
  EPIC_LIST
- **Order.** Two lanes run in parallel: the hub lane 1 → 2 → 3 and the web lane 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8; 5 also waits for 1, 6 for 2; 9 waits for 3, 7 and 8. Epic 4 starts on the prototype's bridge and moves to the hub's edge when epic 1 lands. The blocking edges are on the items.
- **When and who.** The build starts after v0.1.3 ships (the human's decision of 2026-10-14), led by the cland-impl session as v0.1.2 was; the human's questions wait in the inbox.
- **Crew.** As in v0.1.2: Implementers on Codex gpt-6.1-sol, Claude Code roles on claude-sonnet-5-5, the Reviewer pool at 2; `[plan] approval = "none"` except epic 4, whose plan the human approves by hand because it sets the patterns of the web lane.
- **Dogfooding.** Each epic is used as it lands: the edge and the snapshot serve the prototype's shell first; the human steers the rest of the build from the browser once Topics and the inbox land; the phone layouts are tried on the human's phone through their tunnel.
- **Definition of done.** Each epic's demo run by QA on `dev` with a clear verdict, the web phase included in headless Chromium; v0.2.0 released with `cland release --item R-377`; a retrospective artifact; no separate human acceptance pass.

1The card at reading size

E-270's card as the explorer's right part shows it, here alone at the item view's size: the same blocks in the same order as the compact card beside the tree. Enter moves the focus in; the header lists the keys that work thereproposal P21.

2Summary

One to three sentences from the item's ## Summary, written by whoever created it (§3.9). When it is missing, the first paragraph of the first section that is not Parent stands in; for an epic that is its Goal.

3Facts

Status, rank, layer, labels, parent path, lead, claim and holds (§3.9). An epic has no status (v0.1 §3.2), so it derives one from its tasksproposal P2, and its layer is the highest of theirsproposal P15. With leads = [] the Coordinator leads every epic and nothing claims or holds the epic itself, so the claims and holds on its tasks say who is on itproposal P16.

4Progress

For a container, the children's breakdown (§3.9): one cell per task in status order, the counts in words, the same cells per stage. Green done, outlined green ready, amber review and qa, teal in progress, outlined planned, dark backlogproposal P3.

5Plan stage

For an epic only (§3.9): the stage in work with its merged count, lanes and QA policy, the stages around it, the approval. gp opens the plan view (v0.1 §3.13).

6Latest verdict

The latest verdict and its one-line reason (§3.9). An epic's own verdict is the QA demo at its end, so until then the card shows the latest verdict among its tasks and names the taskproposal P17.
